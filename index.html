Work at Elektrobit

Since 2022 I’ve worked across a Linux-based Adaptive AUTOSAR platform and its developer tooling. These are the main areas. The code is proprietary, so the diagrams are schematic.

Adaptive AUTOSAR components

I own production C++14/20 components on the platform, from design through implementation, integration, debugging and maintenance. They talk to the rest of the system through ara::com and SOME/IP, configured with ARXML manifests. Inside: event-driven processing, queues, templates, and smart-pointer ownership.

Secure diagnostics

Diagnostic functions that have to be protected: reading and writing calibration data, accessing ECU configuration, and locking and unlocking the ECU. The cryptography goes through CryptoAPI and Botan.

Access control and storage health

Identity and access management for Adaptive applications, which decides what each application is allowed to use. I also work on eMMC usage monitoring, which tracks how the platform’s flash storage is being used over time.

The Windows development SDK

I hold design ownership of the SDK: architecture, requirements, SwAD/SwDD documentation, integration and maintenance. I led its move from VirtualBox to WSL, which made the development environment roughly 60% faster and reduced reliance on Windows-native third-party packages. I also enabled ARM target debugging from x86 hosts and fixed QEMU networking on Windows.

On site with customers

Requirements and verification

Research

Both theses deal with getting useful results out of imperfect signals and limited hardware.

Model quantization for efficient DMS analysis

Master’s thesis · Tampere University · 2026

Differential mobility spectrometry (DMS) separates ions and produces two-dimensional plots of the result. I trained U-Net models to predict these plots and compared several attention mechanisms. Then I quantized the models to see what they give up in accuracy for a smaller memory footprint and faster inference, which decides whether they can run on embedded hardware.

Removing motion artifacts from PPG signals

Bachelor’s thesis · LUMS · 2021 · repository

Photoplethysmography (PPG) is the optical pulse signal behind most heart-rate sensors, and it falls apart when the wearer moves or perfusion is poor. I wrote a real-time, overlapping-window algorithm in MATLAB that detects and removes these artifacts, which improved heart-rate accuracy.
